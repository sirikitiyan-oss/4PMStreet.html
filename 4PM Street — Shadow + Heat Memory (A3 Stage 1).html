HOW THIS IS CALCULATED
  • A point at height h on flat ground casts a shadow of length L = h / tan(α), where α is the sun’s altitude. The shadow points away from the sun, toward azimuth + 180°.
  • Theory column is computed from that formula using the sun position above. Simulated column is not: the check places an isolated copy of the tree in the simulation, asks the simulation’s own shaded-or-not function about every point along the shadow axis (every 0.02 m), takes the far edge of the shadow, and subtracts the crown radius (the crown is a disk of radius Ø/2 swept from trunk to tip).
  • Direction is tested by scanning a circle of radius L around the trunk every 0.25° and averaging the shaded angles.
  • Tolerance: PASS if |Δ| ≤ max(0.10 m, 1 % of L). Direction PASS if within 1.0°. The two sun algorithms must agree within .
  • What a PASS means: the geometry code reproduces the textbook formula on a flat site. It does not show that the modelled tree resembles a real tree, or that pavement temperatures are correct. That is the job of field data, below.

Field calibration — enter one real measurement

Surface temperature is not human thermal comfort. Infrared thermometers are commonly good to about ±2 °F at best, and the reading depends on the emissivity of the surface and on where you point it. Record your instrument in the notes. Nothing is pre-filled except today’s date, and no demonstration data is stored.

WHAT TO COLLECT (field protocol, short form)
  • Use the same instrument and the same emissivity setting for every reading; write it in the notes.
  • Point the sensor straight down at bare pavement, from the same height, away from paint, cracks and your own shadow.
  • Log the local clock time to the minute. The app converts it to the correct UTC offset (CDT or CST) for the date.
  • For the shadow check: stand a pole or measure a post of known height, then tape the shadow length on level ground at the logged time.
  • Cooling curves have their own tab: one location, one reading every five minutes after shade arrives.

    4PM Street is a simplified design simulation. Until you apply field data, thermal and vegetation parameters are adjustable assumptions, not measurements. Even after calibration, a result is a model prediction of pavement surface temperature on one surface type under one set of conditions, with the measurement error of your thermometer; it is not human thermal comfort, and the proposal button is a rule-based heuristic, not an AI model.
